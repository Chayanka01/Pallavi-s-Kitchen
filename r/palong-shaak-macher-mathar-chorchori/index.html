Palong shaak diye macher mathar chorchori

Fish head

  • Marinate half big or one small rohu/katla fish head with salt, haldi powder and some mustard oil
  • Fry the fish head in hot mustard oil till golden brown

Bhaja moshla

  • Dry roast 7-8 dried red chillies, 2 large bay leaves, 1 tbsp dhaniya seeds and 1/2 tbsp jeera seeds, 1/2 tbsp black pepper, 2 sticks of dalchini, 10-12 laung and 5-6 green elaichi
  • Cool and grind to a fine powder

Chorchori

  • Heat some mustard oil in a pan (you can use the fish head frying oil)
  • Add 1.5 tsp panch phoron, 2 dried red chillies and 1 tejpatta
  • Add one medium onion (thinly sliced) , 2 green chillies (chopped) and a pinch of salt.
  • Fry everything at medium (600 induction) for 4-5 minutes or till the onion is slightly brown
  • Add 15-20 garlic cloves (smashed in a mortar pestle) and fry for 3-4 minutes till it's very slightly golden
  • Add 200g pumpkin (small cubes) and add a splash of water
  • Cover and cook till the pumpkin is soft (stir in between)
  • Add ~200 g spinach leaves (palong shak) finely chopped and mix well
  • Cover and cook for 7-8 minutes till all the saag wilts
  • Open the cover and add 1/2 tsp bhaja moshla
  • Increase flame to medium high (900 induction) and stir the entire sabzi for 5-7 minutes or till the saag becomes dark green
  • Add the fried fish head and mash well using a spoon

NOTE- The sabzi should be like a mish mash i.e. everything should be broken down well together

  • Add 2 whole green chillies, a small pinch of salt, 1 tbsp chopped dhaniya leaves and 1 tbsp fresh mustard oil
  • Cover and turn off the flame
  • Keep covered till serving to preserve the aroma of the raw mustard oil
Background

Macher matha is the fish head, and in a chorchori it goes in near the end, already fried, and is broken up with a spoon until the meat from the head is mixed through the spinach and pumpkin. A chorchori is cooked down until the vegetables lose their shape, so the mash is the intended texture rather than a sign of overcooking.

The bhaja moshla is roasted and ground on its own, and only half a teaspoon of it goes in. The spoonful of raw mustard oil at the end stays under the closed lid until the dish is served.
