Chicken Champaran Kosha (Munuma fusion recipe)

Masala paste

Dry roast

  • Dried red chilli 6
  • 5 green elaichi
  • 1tsp kabab chini
  • 2 to 3 small piece dalchini
  • 8 to 10 laung
  • 1 small blade javitri
  • Cool the spices and grind to a rough powder
  • Add 20-25 cloves garlic and 1 inch ginger to the powdered spices and make a thick paste with little bit of water

Marination

Marinate cleaned curry cut skinless chicken (500g) with:

  • lemon juice (one lemon)
  • mustard oil 4 to 5 tbsp
  • salt
  • haldi (little)
  • mirchi powder (1 tsp)
  • black pepper powder (1tsp)
  • onion powder (1 tbsp)
  • garlic powder (2 tbsp)
  • garlic cloves 10-12
  • sliced onion 1 big
  • 2 broken green chillies
  • whole spices (3 green elaichi, 2 bay leaves, 6 laung, 1 inch dalchini)
  • grated nutmeg
  • MASALA PASTE (recipe given above)
  • 3 whole garlics washed

Potato (optional)

  • If you want to add potatoes to the dish, cut them in big pieces and fry in mustard oil with some salt and kashmiri chilli powder
  • Take the fried potatoes and add in a pan with boiling water
  • boil the potatoes till fully cooked

Cooking

  • Heat mustard oil in kadhai and fry 1 big onion thinly sliced till brown.
  • Let it cool
  • Add the marinated chicken alongwith the marinade
  • cover with lid and a chakla or any heavy weight and cook at 900 induction for 10 min.
  • uncover and stir, cover again, cook for 10 -15 min till all liquid dries.
  • uncover and add a bit of water, add tomatoes cut in big chunks, mix everything, cover and cook till chicken is fully cooked, masala roasted nicely.
  • Add a little more water, reduce it a bit.
  • OPTIONAL STEP- add the prepared potatoes (potato prep written above)
  • Add 1 tsp ghee, 2 whole green chillies and little sugar
  • Cover and take off the heat and keep covered till serving
Background

Champaran meat, also called ahuna, handi meat or batlohi, comes from the Champaran region of Bihar. It is cooked in an earthenware handi whose mouth is sealed with kneaded dough and set over a low wood fire, so the meat cooks in its own steam with mustard oil, ghee, garlic, ginger, onion and curd. No water is added at any point.

Kosha is a Bengali method rather than a dish. The masala is fried down with very little liquid, water returned a spoonful at a time, and the browned residue scraped back in until the fat separates.

The two methods pull in opposite directions on water, and this recipe uses both. A lid weighted with a chakla stands in for the dough seal through the first dry stage, where all the liquid is driven off. The tomatoes and the small additions of water that follow are the kosha half.
