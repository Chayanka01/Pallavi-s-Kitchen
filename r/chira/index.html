Chira

STEPS

  1. 1Slice one big potato into small cubes
  2. 2Slice one big onion into thin long slices.
  3. 3Cut 2-3 green chillies into small pieces.
  4. 4Heat 2 big spoon (tablespoon) refined oil and one small spoon (teaspoon) ghee in the pan / Kadhai. (600 V induction, 2-3 min)
  5. 5Add the potato cubes and a pinch of salt. Fry till potatoes are cooked (soft inside) and crispy outside. Note :- Stir with a spoon in between to prevent potatoes from sticking
  6. 6Push the potatoes to one side of the pan and add the onions to the empty side. Don't stir now.
  7. 7Add 1 tejpatta (bay leaf), 4-5 loung (cloves), 2-3 elichi, 1 small stick dalchini (cinnamon), green chilli slices
  8. 8Mix everything. Add some kaju and kishmish if available.
  9. 7Let everything cook at 400 V induction while you go to prepare the chira.
  10. 8Take the big steel bowl and add a little more than half bowl chira.
  11. 9Wash in tap water for 2-3 times. Drain out all water. Mix the chira in the steel bowl itself to redistribute leftover moisture. Chira will become softer.
  12. 10Add the chira to the pan. Increase temperature to 600 V.
  13. 11Add 3/4th big spoon (tablespoon) sugar and 1 big spoon (tablespoon) Jharna Ghee.
  14. 12Mix well. Turn off induction and Serve hot!! Enjoy :)

The numbering runs 1 to 8 and then restarts at 7, as on the page.

Background

Chira in Bengali, sira in Assamese, poha further west: rice that has been dehusked, parboiled or soaked, dried, roasted and rolled flat. It is sold thin, medium and thick, and the thickness determines what it can be used for, the thinner grades going into cooked dishes and sweets.

Washing it and then leaving it to stand lets the water remaining in the flakes spread evenly through them. Soaking it instead makes it collapse.
