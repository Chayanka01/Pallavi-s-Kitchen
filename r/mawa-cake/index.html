Mawa cake recipe (yields 500g cake)

  • take the round patila (baking dish) and brush all sides with jharna ghee
  • cut out a round A4 paper in the size of the bottom of the patila and brush it with ghee and add to the bottom of the patila
  • put a thick layer of salt in the pressure cooker and add a stand.
  • take out the whistle and gasket from the lid of pressure cooker and keep it to pre-heat at 900 (induction)
  • soften 60g amul salted butter on double boiled, add 35 g granulated (normal) sugar and whisk till white and creamy
  • add 1 egg and whisk till incorporated and creamy
  • add 80g amul gold condensed milk (room temperature) and whisk well to mix
  • crumble 120g amul khoya (room temperature) and add it , mix well
  • add 80g amul taaza milk, and mix well
  • in a separate bowl, mix 120g maida, 1/2 tsp baking soda and 1.5 tsp baking powder
  • add to the butter sugar khoya mixture and mix gently
  • add the cake mixture to the patila (baking dish) , tap on the counter to make the level even and put it in the pre-heated cooker
  • bake at 900 for 10 min and then at 600 for 25-30 min , till the sides are brown, top is slightly brown and a knife inserted in the center comes out clean
  • take the cake out from the cooker and let it cool on the counter for 1-2 hr before cutting and serving
Background

Mawa cake belongs to the Irani cafés of Bombay, run by Zoroastrian families who came from Persia and settled in the city; the oldest of the cafés still trading have been selling it for roughly a century. Khoya, milk reduced until it solidifies, gives the cake a close and faintly grainy crumb quite unlike a butter sponge.

The method is a piece of ovenless baking. A pressure cooker with the gasket and whistle removed becomes the oven, a thick bed of salt spreads the heat and keeps the base from scorching, and a stand holds the tin clear of the metal.
