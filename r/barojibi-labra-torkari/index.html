Barojibi (barui) special labra torkari

Bhaja moshla

Dry roast and grind

  • coriander seeds 1 tbsp
  • radhuni 1/4 tbsp
  • mouri 1/4 tbsp
  • dried red chillies 2 medium
  • tejpatta 1 small

Cooking

  • Heat 3 to 4 tbsp mustard oil.
  • Add 8-10 urad daler bori, fry for 1 to 2 minutes and keep aside
  • In the same oil add 2 dried red chillies, 1 tejpatta and 1 tbsp panch foron (mixture of fennel seeds, cumin seeds, nigella seeds, radhuni seeds/black mustard seeds, fenugreek seeds)
  • Add grated ginger, 2 to 3 slit green chillies, fry well
  • Add 3 to 4 tbsp freshly grated coconut and fry till light golden (optional step)
  • Add medium cubed potatoes, small cubed sweet potatoes, cubed brinjal, half moon slices of rigde gourd (zika/jhinge), small cubed pumpkin, small slices of broad bean (sheem)
  • Add salt and haldi powder
  • Cover and cook on medium to low medium heat (900 or 600 induction) with stirring in between

Note- The vegetables will start getting soft slowly and they have to be mashed as they get soft. In labra no individual vegetable should be visible

  • when all the vegetables are cooked and mashed well, add the fried daler bori, 1tbsp ghee, 1tbsp sugar, 2 to 3 green chillies and 2tbsp bhaja moshla.
  • Mix well, cover and turn off heat and serve
Background

The Barujibi, also called Barui, are a Bengali community whose hereditary occupation is the cultivation and trade of betel leaf; the name comes from the Sanskrit words for betel and for livelihood. They belong to the Nabasakh grouping and were found throughout undivided Bengal, most numerously in the Dacca district.

Labra is a niramish dish, cooked without onion or garlic, tempered with panch phoron and ginger and served with khichuri at Lokkhi pujo and Durga pujo. Published recipes generally cook the vegetables in stages so that each keeps its shape. The version here calls for the opposite, mashing them as they soften until none can be told from another.

Panch phoron is given here as taking either radhuni or black mustard as its fifth seed, a substitution recorded in Bengali cooking. The bori are urad lentil paste dried in the sun, one of the ways dal is kept through the year.
