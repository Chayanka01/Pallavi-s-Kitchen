Pumpkin Fish curry recipe (mishti kumro diye rui macher jhol)

Masala paste

  • Dry roast 5 to 6 dried red chillies, 1 tsp jeera, half tsp dhaniya seeds till fragrant
  • Turn off heat and let the kadhai cool down a bit
  • Add half tsp black mustard seeds. Let the thing cool down completely
  • Grind into fine powder
  • Add whole garlic cloves (18-20) and make a fine paste (DO NOT ADD TOO MUCH WATER)

Fish and Vegetables

  • marinate rui/katla pieces (350- 400g) with salt, haldi and mustard oil
  • Heat mustard oil till it smokes, reduce temperature a bit and fry till reddish brown on both sides
  • In the same oil, fry medium cubes of pumpkin and medium cubed potatoes till almost fully cooked

Curry

  • In the same oil in which the fish and vegetables were fried, add half tsp methi and 1tsp jeera as phoron
  • Add 1.5 medium onions (or 2 small onions) finely chopped and 10 whole garlic cloves and fry well till onion is reddish
  • add the MASALA PASTE and fry well till oil separates
  • add the fried pumpkin and alu and coat with masala and fry for 2 to 3 min
  • add some water , mix and cover and cook till vegetables are fully cooked
  • Add 3/4th glass water and boil well till oil separates from jhol
  • Add fried fish pieces and boil for 5 min or till gravy reduced a bit
  • adjust for salt. Garnish with little bit chopped dhaniya leaves and serve
Background

Rui (rohu) and katla are the carps most commonly cooked in Bengali households. A jhol is the lighter, everyday form of fish curry, thinner and less heavily spiced than a kalia.

The masala is built in two stages. Chillies, cumin and coriander are dry- roasted, then the pan is taken off the heat and allowed to cool before the mustard seeds go in. Roasted mustard turns bitter, so residual warmth is used in place of direct heat. The methi and jeera dropped into hot oil at the start of the curry is the tempering step known as phoron.
