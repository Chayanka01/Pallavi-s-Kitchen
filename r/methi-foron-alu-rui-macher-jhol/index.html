Methi foron diye alu rui macher jhol

Fish and potatoes

  • Marinate 500 g rui (rohu) fish with salt haldi powder and mustard oil
  • Cut the potatoes in vertical wedges and boil them till they're 80-90% done, drain them and air dry for few minutes.
  • heat 8-10 tbsp mustard oil in a kadhai and fry the fish in batches till they're reddish brown and slightly crispy
  • In the same oil, fry the boiled potato wedges till they're slightly golden on all sides

Masala paste

  • Dry roast 5 to 6 dried red chillies and 1.5 tbsp jeera seeds
  • Cool down and make a smooth paste

Curry

  • In the fish frying oil, add 1 tsp methi, 1/2 tsp jeera, 1 medium tejpatta and 1 chopped green chilli
  • Add 2 medium onions thinly sliced and fry till onion is slightly golden
  • Add the masala paste and fry till oil separates

Note- If the masala sticks too much sprinkle 1 to 2 tsp water and then fry well

  • Add 1 medium tomato (medium chunks) , pinch of sugar and cover and cook at low medium (600 induction) for 3 to 4 minutes till the tomato is soft
  • Add 1 glass (~250 mL) water and let it come to a vigorous boil on high heat (1200 induction).
  • Boil till the water reduces by one third
  • Add the fried potatoes and fried fish, cover and cook for 5 to 7 minutes

Note- Turn the fish over once in between so that both sides of the fish get exposed to the curry)

  • Turn off the heat , garnish with 1-2 whole raw green chillies and serve
Background

Methi dropped whole into the hot fish oil is the tempering, and the curry takes its name from it. Fenugreek darkens fast and turns bitter if it goes too far, and that edge runs through the jhol.

The masala is only dry-roasted red chilli and cumin, without the mustard seed of the Pumpkin Fish Curry elsewhere in the archive. Parboiling the potato wedges and letting them air-dry before frying gives them a skin that holds together in the gravy.
