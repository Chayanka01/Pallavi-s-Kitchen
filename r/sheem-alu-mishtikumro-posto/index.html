Sheem Alu mishtikumro Posto

Posto paste

  • Soak 2-3 tbsp posto seeds in water alongwith 2 green chillies and 2 dried red chillies
  • Strain the soaking liquid using a strainer and make a thick paste with little bit of water

Sabzi

  • Heat 5-6 tbsp mustard oil in a kadhai and add 1/2 tsp methi seeds and 1 tsp kala jeera (kalojeere/kalonji) seeds
  • Add 1 small potato (small cubes) and 1 katori pumpkin cut into small cubes
  • Add 1 tsp salt and fry for 5 minutes
  • Add 2 small onions (finely chopped) and 1 chopped green chilli
  • Fry everything at low-medium (600 induction) for 5-7 mins or till the onions are translucent
  • Add 250 g Sheem (broad beans) (string removed and cut into small pieces) and fry at medium high (900 induction) for 5 minutes
  • Cover with a lid and put the flame to low medium (600 induction) and cook for 15-20 minutes with stirring in between till the sheem is 80% cooked.
  • Add some haldi powder, mix and cover the vegetables and cook at low (400 induction) for 10 minutes till all the vegetables are fully cooked
  • Add the posto chilli paste and cover and cook at low medium (600 induction) for 5 to 7 minutes
  • Open the cover and incorporate the brown bits in the sabzi and mash a bit of the vegetables for a better texture
  • Turn heat to medium high (900 induction) and DO NOT COVER now, keep stirring and cooking for 5 to 7 minutes till the sabzi comes together nicely and the posto paste releases nutty aroma
  • turn off heat, top with one whole green chilli and 2 tbsp raw mustard oil and let it stay covered off the heat for 10 minutes before serving.
Background

Posto is poppy seed, and its place in Bengali cooking is tied to the opium trade. After 1757 the East India Company turned large parts of western Bengal, Bihar and Orissa over to poppy. The food writer Pritha Sen traces posto to the farming households on that land, who took up the seeds left over as waste when their fields no longer fed them. From there it spread through Bankura, Birbhum, Burdwan and Midnapore and across the Padma into eastern Bengal.

The paste goes in late. The final minutes uncovered on high heat drive off its raw taste and bring out the nuttiness, and the dish then rests covered, off the heat, under raw mustard oil.
