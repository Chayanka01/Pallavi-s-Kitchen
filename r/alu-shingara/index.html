Alu shingara recipe

Dough

  • Mix 150g maida with a tiny pinch of baking soda, 1/2 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp black salt, 1/2 tsp kalonji (kalojeere) seeds and 1/2 tsp ajwain seeds
  • In a pan heat 35g dalda till melted but NOT smoking
  • Add the hot dalda to the flour and rub together till the texture is of wet sand and a clump of the mixture holds shape
  • Add cold water 1 to 2 tbsp at a time and bring together the maida (the dough should be fairly tight)

NOTE - DO NOT knead the dough too much and the dough would need around 8-10 tbsp water at Max

  • Cover and rest for at least 30 minutes

Stuffing

  • Boil 6 small or 4 medium potatoes till soft, drain from water and mash and let it cool
  • In a kadhai heat some oil (3 tbsp) and dalda (1 to 2 tbsp) at medium heat (600 induction)
  • Add 1 tbsp jeera, 1/2 tbsp hing and let it sputter
  • Add a fistful of green peas and let it cook for 5-6 minutes
  • Add 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp black salt, 1 tbsp kasuri methi and a coarse paste of (1/2 inch piece of ginger and 3 green chillies).
  • Mix and cook for 5 more minutes
  • Turn off the heat and add 1/2 tsp haldi powder, 1 tsp mirchi powder, 1 tsp amchur powder
  • Turn heat back on to medium (600 induction) and fry the spices for 1 minute
  • Add the cooled mashed potatoes and mix well
  • Increase heat to medium high (900 induction) and cook for 7-8 minutes or till the potato mixture is completely dry
  • Take the mixture out from the kadhai and let it cool completely

Samosa formation and frying

  • Take the dough ball and knead it one more time
  • Divide the dough into 3 equal pieces
  • Dust some dry maida onto the chakla and roll an oblong oval shape of around 2mm thickness (not too thin)
  • Cut the oval on the short axis and line the straight edge with water
  • Bring the edges together to make a cone and seal it tight
  • Stuff the potato mixture using a spoon and fill it well
  • Wet the circular edges with water and make a fold opposite to the seam side and pinch the entire samosa shut

NOTE -Do not overfill the samosas

  • Heat enough oil+dalda in a pan to submerge the samosas on low heat (400 induction)
  • Drop the samosas (Do not overcrowd) and fry at low heat (400 induction) for 12-15 minutes by flipping carefully in between till it's golden and flaky

NOTE- If any surface of the samosa is peeking above the oil, just take a spoon and baste gently with hot oil on top

  • Remove the samosas on a steel plate to preserve crispiness
Background

Shingara is the Bengali name for the samosa. The pastry is shortened with dalda, melted and rubbed into the flour while hot until the mixture holds a clump like wet sand, then brought together with very little water and barely kneaded. Fried slowly on the lowest setting for twelve to fifteen minutes, the crust blisters and cooks through before it colours.

The filling is cooked until completely dry and cooled before it goes in, so no steam softens the pastry from inside. Amchur sours it without adding any liquid.
